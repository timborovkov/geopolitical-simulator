# Regional market integration contract

11 November 2026. **Engineering design; no regional market implementation or calibration result is claimed.** This records MT-01 of the [market plan](markets-and-trade-design.md). MT-02 must supply exact economic rules and MT-03 must supply a reproducible fixture before MT-04 implements settlement. The [requirement register](implementation-register.csv) and global priority order remain unchanged. All new economic values will be authored assumptions, not historical observations.

## Parent and compatibility

Use optional **rules 40 / snapshot 41** as the parent. Regional trade needs its real wage liabilities, earnings protection and equipment accounting; introducing a parallel approximation of those obligations would obscure merchant capital. Reserve **rules 41 / snapshot 42** for the connected-market extension, subject to checking that those numbers are still unused when implementation begins. This document does not make those formats loadable. The recommended default remains finance rules 38 and its existing starter.

The new content section is mandatory only for the new rules. Its live state is absent, and omitted from serialization, for archived rules. No old content is rewritten, no old save is upgraded implicitly, and an old campaign cannot activate markets midway. Exact-pack loading must check both rules and content fingerprint. Unknown, mismatched or incomplete market definitions fail before creating a world. The full archived recovery suite must retain its recorded hashes.

## Identity, ownership and units

Start with three fictional countries, each containing one explicitly authored market region: farming, mining and industrial port. Region IDs are independent of country IDs and map feature IDs. A total one-to-one country/region mapping is an explicit first-version validation restriction; later subnational markets require allocation of residents, production, fiscal obligations and assets before this restriction can be lifted. A reference polygon does not create a market or transfer sovereignty.

Use distinct typed IDs for region, link, route and shipment. A route has a receiving region, supplying region, ordered link path and good. A link has endpoint IDs, operator country, shared daily capacity, transport fee and access state. Validate a path's continuity, endpoints and absence of repeated regions before accepting it. Resolve definitions by ID, and use stable IDs for allocation, never input vector order or screen order.

An owner reference names an existing country and account. Initially the domestic aggregate `Firms` account owns its country's civilian output, private industrial stocks and imported commercial cargo. Producer and merchant activities share this owner's real wallet; they do not receive duplicate starting capital. Households retain their separate cohort cash and ownership claims. Treasury reserves and public accounts keep their existing owners. Do not create individual merchant companies or nominal company wallets in this slice.

Use the existing typed Food, Energy and industrial good quantities. MT-02 must define each tradable good's physical lot size, transport weight and monetary quotation, with checked conversions and a positive cash cost for every paid lot. Food is not industrial biomass; civilian energy is not industrial fuel. Nonstorable contracted services cannot become cargo. Keep deposits at their extraction location. A complete industrial input chain may be connected in stages, but no recipe may consume an undefined good or create unrecorded inputs.

The country civilian stock fields remain authoritative totals in the one-region prototype. Regional reports project those totals; they do not maintain another independently mutable copy. Country production stores remain authoritative for industrial quantities. Transit quantities have exactly one shipment owner and are excluded from both regions' available stock until arrival.

## Cash and business accounting

Today, `Accounts` and `World::transfer` move cash inside one country and validation requires each country's cash total to equal its opening total. That invariant cannot describe a real cross-border payment. Extend it only for the new rules, using explicit double-entry records:

- A cash leg records campaign day, transaction/shipment identity, source country/account, destination country/account, positive amount and purpose. Domestic legs continue through the existing transfer boundary.
- A cross-border leg atomically debits the payer and credits the recipient. Maintain cumulative receipts and payments per country; its cash total equals opening cash plus cumulative cross-border receipts minus cumulative cross-border payments. The sum of net cross-border positions is zero, and world cash equals world opening cash. Retaining only a 30-day inspection ledger must never discard the cumulative accounting balances.
- Record exporter receipts, importer purchase cost, transport-operator receipts and collecting treasury tariff receipts against their actual owners. Do not settle foreign purchases against both countries' separate bounded `External` accounts. Existing abstract external flows remain separately labelled parent-model flows.
- Update both countries' business cash bridges for a firm payment, and update the appropriate inventory cost basis. Buying cargo is an asset conversion, not immediate final consumption or a second wage expense. On delivery it remains inventory; recognize cost on actual use, sale or explicit loss. Cross-border cash movement alone is not GDP. Domestic aggregate firms do not pay themselves for internal stock transfers.
- One shared reservation book constrains every competing use of an owner's cash. Merchant capital excludes protected undelivered public-client funds, outstanding payroll liabilities, already reserved equipment payments and the specified liquidity buffer. Neither expected resale proceeds, unpaid receivables nor cargo book value can fund a purchase. MT-02 must define the buffer and prove its interaction with the parent's distribution and investment protections.

Do not weaken validation for archived rules. Check every sum, quantity conversion and recipient balance before committing a transaction. A failed debit, overflow, missing recipient or inconsistent cost basis must leave both countries, cargo, capacity, ledger and command history unchanged.

## Daily integration points

The parent advances countries in ID order and calls `settle` separately. Cross-border clearing inside that loop would let earlier countries spend receipts or consume arrivals before later countries and would make route competition depend on incidental settlement order. Add world-wide barriers for the new rules; leave the archived loop unchanged.

| Phase | Existing hook and new boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Start of day | Preserve cost-day initialization, clock advance, equipment dating and demographic review. Deliver yesterday's dispatched shipments into their destination owner's actual stock before any country's settlement. Arrival is not another purchase, tariff or operating receipt. |
| Government and contracted deliveries | Preserve statutory, consent, borrowing, project, reserve and industrial-contract reviews. Settle existing funded commitments against actual funds and inputs. A new merchant cannot appropriate protected public materials or replace a promised contract delivery. |
| Country economy | Preserve existing output, fixed wage assessment/payment, non-wage income, tax, public payments, household consumption and auxiliary-production resource limits. Current labor funding still excludes payment of old wage arrears. Industrial production consumes inputs once; nonstorable services and protected public construction claims retain their documented treatment. |
| World commercial clearing | After every country has settled, freeze regional observations. Determine offers only from actual unprotected closing stocks. Resolve all regions' merchant orders against that same snapshot with shared money, demand, stock and link-capacity reservations. Transfer actual purchase, tariff and transport payments and move goods into next-day shipments. A receipt from this phase cannot finance another purchase in the same phase. |
| Next-day prices and targets | Update regional prices once from the specified snapshot and realized clearing diagnostics. MT-02 must reconcile this with the existing `next_market_price` call: suppress or replace the corresponding parent update for new rules, never compound two updates. The daily settlement report retains the price actually charged. Update merchant targets from observed results, not assumed sale of cargo in transit. |
| End of day | Validate cash, inventories, production conversions, claims, reservations and cost bases; evaluate/report outcomes and retain inspection history. Monthly country AI runs only after the world commercial phase, issuing the same commands as the player. |

MT-02 must specify the exact price supply/demand inputs, whether a quote refers to a physical unit or a lot, civilian affordability, protected-stock deductions, and the placement of private payouts/investment relative to commercial reservations. These are exit conditions for economic implementation, not permission to guess while coding. The initial design retains parent domestic settlement before exports; household essentials are not granted an undocumented new absolute priority over every industrial/public use.

At dispatch, ownership transfers to the receiving country's firms, purchase cash leaves that owner, and goods leave origin available stock. The shipment arrives on the following campaign day. A later road closure stops new dispatches and does not erase already purchased cargo. Any future exceptional loss must name the quantity, owner, cost write-off and compensation source. The prototype does not infer losses from map geometry or military events.

## Commands, visibility and clients

Import tariffs, access changes and infrastructure proposals use typed shared commands, authority checks and normal command history. A controller acts only for its own government. Infrastructure must use paid delayed policy delivery, with material and service requirements; an inspector cannot directly increase a link's capacity. MT-02/MT-05 must assign link control and identify who can consent to a cross-border upgrade. Foreign country AI uses public observations plus its own private reports and the same command boundary.

Public regional reports may expose public prices, publicly observed shortages, link access and aggregated flow. Detailed owner cash, protected funds, cost basis, route profitability, pending private orders and cohort finances are available only to the controlling country. Do not derive a foreign private wallet from an unrestricted world report. Core constructs visibility-filtered projections; desktop and headless do not recalculate settlement or evade this boundary.

Extend the existing runner with inspection and reproducible scripted commands; do not build a second simulator with different arithmetic. Both clients load the same pinned fixture. Native map and chart work follows the headless accounting and shock gates. Initial UI must explain a closed route separately from an unprofitable or cash-constrained route, and distinguish ordered, dispatched, arrived and consumed quantities.

## State, rollback and proof obligations

New persisted state includes regional price/observation history, route targets and persistence counters, shipment IDs and cargo cost bases, link access/capacity commitments, and cumulative cross-border cash positions. Retain the deterministic next-ID counters needed for replay. Temporary reservation books must be empty at the transaction boundary and cannot be serialized as spendable duplicate balances.

Advance and apply remain transactional through the existing public boundaries. Validate content identity and the pre-state first; build a staged whole-world change; validate the resulting state; publish it only on success. A cross-country transaction cannot commit one country's half early. Exact save/resume tests must stop with cargo in transit and pending infrastructure/authority decisions, then compare the continued world, event links, balances and canonical save bytes with uninterrupted replay.

MT-04 must test: archived campaign hashes; permutation of definition order; shared capacity in both directions and across goods; competing routes sharing one payer and buyer demand; no spending commercial receipts again on the same day; protected wages/client funds; zero and tiny quantities; insufficient cash; unsold arrivals; closure during transit; reopen; tariff reconciliation; invalid content/state rollback; foreign privacy; and whole-world plus per-owner goods/cash reconciliation. Baseline windows and numerical stability tolerances must be declared in MT-03 before running the scenario. A passing unit test is not evidence of baseline stability or player comprehension.

## Remaining readiness work

This contract fixes the parent, one-region boundary, ownership, cross-border accounting, staged settlement and compatibility approach. MT-02 remains open: precise utilization response and insolvency/re-entry, lot arithmetic and tariff collection, price inputs, reservation allocation/ties and protected liquidity need worked executable cases. MT-03 remains open: author the full connected recipe fixture and opening reconciliation, then declare baseline/shock tolerances. Neither task is satisfied by this document, and no regional market requirement is complete.

Two existing arithmetic boundaries need explicit MT-02 decisions. `consumption::quoted_food_budget` splits an essential basket into food and other spending, then `charged_food` charges for the delivered fraction of affordable need. Its basis-point price index is not a whole-money price per physical unit. A wholesale quote cannot silently treat it as one or promise a destination resale value the household settlement never pays. Define how the new quote maps to actual household charges, and test poor and wealthy cohorts buying the same good before freezing the fixture. Likewise, `distribute_business_cash` protects actual trailing 30-day firm payments. Include cross-border firm payments in that same dated liquidity calculation exactly once; ignoring them would overstate available merchant capital, while adding mirrored local payments would double-count costs.
