# Initial content pack: 1 January 2000

The default `initial-2000-commitments.json` uses **content schema 6 / rules 6**, ID `initial-2000-commitments-v6`. The preserved constitutional (`initial-2000-constitutions.json`, rules 5), financing (`initial-2000-finance.json`, rules 4), market (`initial-2000-markets.json`, rules 3) and classic (`initial-2000.json`, rules 2) packs retain their exact original definitions. All five share the same 15 countries, 34 parties/governing bodies, eight opening regime configurations, four household groups per country, seven policies and six reference facts. Later packs add separately attributed game assumptions for prices, borrowing, constitutional transitions and policy procedures. Both clients embed all five for offline play. The default loader resolves a save's exact content hash and retains its rules; `--content FILE` pins a validated local replacement. Unknown hashes require the exact original file.

Rules 2/3/4/5/6 write snapshot formats 3/4/5/6/7 respectively; classic rules 2 also accept compatible format-2 snapshots. Loading never silently upgrades the rules. Version-1 packs/saves remain unsupported and are rejected without modification.

## Coverage and identity

The UK, US, France, Germany, Russia, China, India, Japan, Brazil, South Africa, Saudi Arabia, Iran, Cuba, North Korea and Myanmar are selectable. Other countries, disputed/dependent territories, subnational geography, detailed cabinets, treaties, organization memberships, military formations and technology trees are not yet represented. This is not a complete 2000 world database.

Historical country, capital, executive and party names remain a **starter identity curation, not a verified historical database**. Executive denotes the modeled governing authority, not necessarily the constitutional head of state: Jiang Zemin is general secretary, Ali Khamenei supreme leader, Vladimir Putin acting president and Kim Jong Il general secretary. Blair and Jospin are heads of government. Full dated office terms, per-office citations, national electoral laws and independent identity review remain outstanding.

The `identity-notes` source ID records these limitations. Candidate reference families include the [Wikidata office-holder model](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:WikiProject_Heads_of_state_and_government/Data_Model), national government archives and election authorities in the [world-data specification](../docs/world-data-specification.md). These pointers do not claim a completed import or certification of historical terms. No restricted political dataset is bundled.

The Saudi royal governing council, Iranian clerical/reformist blocs and Myanmar governing military council are scenario governing-body representations, not assertions that they are ordinary parties or historical 100-seat legislatures. Seats, ideology labels and regular election timing are authored. Competitive elections first occur at day 730 and recur every 1460 days; parliamentary confidence can schedule an earlier election. Future cabinets, leadership replacements and decisions are simulated outcomes, with no guarantee that actual later events occur.

## Dated population and GDP observations

Six fields have per-observation provenance: **2000 population and current-USD GDP for GBR, CHN and SAU**. They come from pinned [datasets/population](https://github.com/datasets/population) and [datasets/gdp](https://github.com/datasets/gdp) mirrors. The mirrors identify World Bank population indicator `SP.POP.TOTL` and World Bank/OECD GDP indicator `NY.GDP.MKTP.CD`.

[Reference observations](reference-observations.json) retains original numeric strings, observation periods, units, applied values, transformations, source revisions, full-file SHA-256 hashes, attribution and limitations. The game embeds the same fact records and exposes them in the overview and terminal `data` inspector. Countries with these observations use `quality: mixed_observed_and_authored`; other values retain authored defaults.

**These are published mirror observations, not independently reconciled primary-source facts.** The World Bank API and national sources were not rechecked. Historical revisions can differ substantially. The Saudi population observation in this pinned mirror differs from the earlier authored seed; the source is recorded without claiming an alternative series has been validated. Annual population is a bookmark proxy, not a 1 January census. Annual GDP is truncated from current USD to million USD for the opening 2000 game calibration. No PPP conversion is applied.

Attribution: World Bank; the GDP mirror also credits OECD. GDP mirror metadata declares CC BY 4.0; population mirror metadata declares ODC PDDL. Upstream World Bank-produced data generally uses CC BY 4.0, so upstream attribution/conditions are retained rather than assuming the mirror can waive them. No restricted third-party political data is included.

Rebuild with Python's standard library:

```sh
python3 scripts/import_reference_data.py
```

For offline regeneration, pass `--population FILE --gdp FILE` using the pinned CSVs. Online and offline files must match recorded SHA-256 digests. Missing or duplicate observations fail; absent data never becomes zero. The script updates classic-pack reference population/GDP, source metadata and facts, leaving other fields intact. Run `python3 scripts/build_synthetic_world.py` afterward to rebuild the derived starter variants from those committed inputs; validation detects stale derived packs. Rust validation checks source/country references, observation units and agreement between applied facts and actual seed values. Full raw series are not bundled.

## Authored models and units

**All remaining economic values and coefficients are authored gameplay approximations**, including debt, cash, taxes, spending, capacities, group profiles, political approval, council thresholds, marker coordinates and ideology profiles. The source ID `authored-balance-v1` identifies the original calibration; new household/government definitions are original scenario content. Historical observations initialize the game; they are not causal coefficients.

Money uses millions of constant-2000 USD-equivalent game credits. Currency codes are identity metadata, not exchange-rate behavior. Annual GDP is an opening calibration, not a measured evolving national account. Population uses integer persons. Daily food/energy use modeled units per thousand persons, not empirical tonnes/GWh. Rates use integer percentage points; program capital costs use basis points of opening GDP. Positive divisions truncate; largest-remainder allocation preserves exact group totals and breaks ties by stable definition order. Small economies have visible rounding effects.

Food and energy have distinct nonnegative Rust quantity types across stocks and physical flows. Checked arithmetic rejects overflow and invalid ratios; rational scaling uses a wide intermediate and rounds down. Their JSON representation remains an integer, preserving version-2 snapshots and canonical replay results. Rates, distances and time still require further dimensional typing.

Four disjoint household classifications partition population and cash: working, public-service, transfer-dependent and asset-owning households. Population, income, savings and working-age shares are authored, not measured census or income-distribution observations. Public-service payments go to the public-service group; welfare goes 80% to transfer-dependent and 20% to working households. Private income follows authored shares. Group taxes, purchases, food delivery, employment and arrears are inspectable. Regions, age transitions, skills, complete labor markets and an endogenous price system are not yet implemented.

Living-standard indicators are read-only calculations over these disjoint group records. Income below the essential basket is financial pressure and can be offset by saved cash; actual food delivery is separate. Cash/basket days assume today's basket cost and ignore future income. The Gini is population-weighted from group-mean disposable income, rounded down to basis points; it excludes within-group inequality and is undefined for zero total income, negative disposable group income or income assigned to a zero-person group. These are authored-model outcomes, not survey poverty or inequality estimates. Before the first settlement, clients withhold realized indicators. No authoritative save state or accounting rule changes for these queries.

The external market is a separate counterpart per country, not a network between selectable states. It starts with three times opening GDP in cash, supplies up to 25% of energy needs and 35% of food needs, buys service exports worth up to 15% of realized output and lends subject to cash and debt limits. It can exhaust its funds. This is bounded trade/lending, not complete international finance.

Government definitions specify decision route, backing threshold, consent delay, council threshold and optional leadership-review interval. The UK reference uses 51 parliamentary support and 14-day consent; China uses elite/cadre backing 55/50 and 14-day consent; Saudi Arabia uses elite/council backing 50/45, immediate decrees and a council condition on large spending increases. These and their review intervals are authored institutional mechanisms, not exact constitutional reproductions. See [governing-term scope](../docs/initial-game.md) for consent, reservation, election and succession rules.

The selectable fiscal/supply crisis overrides only the selected country's policy/capacity/approval calibration; it retains the same population/GDP observations. Its scenario ID is saved and replayed. Seven programs cover income tax, services, welfare, energy, food, administration and support negotiations. Metadata specifies parameter bounds, base delays, capital costs and affected groups; Rust owns behavior. Unknown policy behavior is rejected.

## Map asset and original content

`land-110m.json` derives from [Natural Earth 1:110m land](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/blob/master/geojson/ne_110m_land.geojson), downloaded 3 November 2026. Natural Earth declares its raster/vector data [public domain](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/). Attribution: Natural Earth. The transformation retains polygon outer rings, drops holes and rounds longitude/latitude to three decimals. The client triangulates once at startup. This is geographical land display only: no sovereignty, historical border or territorial claim is asserted. Country markers are authored and do not define control.

The remaining scenario content is original authored game data. No third-party flags, portraits, proprietary game text or restricted political database is included. Dependency licenses are available through the crates referenced in Cargo.lock; release distribution needs the appropriate dependency notices.

`country-outlines-110m.json` contains selectable reference outlines for the 15 starter countries, compiled from Natural Earth v3.3.0, revision `aa15d45ca649047105c9fd38c5d58e95ada27fca`. The [asset manifest](country-outlines-manifest.json) pins each raw SHP/DBF/PRJ digest and URL, the compiled digest, public-domain terms, attribution, transformation and limitations. All exterior rings, islands and interior holes are preserved; coordinates are rounded to 0.001 degrees. The desktop triangulates with holes and picks geographic polygons independently of simulation state. Tests cover overseas parts, the antimeridian, omitted countries and the Lesotho hole in South Africa's outline. Marker fallback cannot select the surrounding country through that hole.

This source release is **not certified as a complete 2000 boundary layer**. It assigns the Crimea sample to Ukraine and treats Taiwan separately from CHN; these checks do not validate every disputed or historical boundary. Only the 15 roster records are drawn/selectable. Other territory remains neutral land, and tiny islands omitted by the source scale remain accessible through search. Display geometry creates no legal sovereignty, occupation, recognition or simulation region. Administrative boundaries, historical date effectiveness, lakes/rivers and terrain remain future work.

Rebuild with `python3 scripts/import_country_geometry.py`. For offline rebuilding, pass `--source-dir DIRECTORY` containing the pinned `ne_110m_admin_0_countries.shp`, `.dbf` and `.prj`; byte mismatches fail. `--validate` checks committed geometry, roster, rings and manifest checksum offline without downloading or writing. The compiler uses Python's standard library. JSON uses LF endings so checksums remain stable across platforms.

## Fictional rules fixture

`synthetic-test.json` is an independent, entirely authored three-country pack: Aurora Republic, Boreal Union and Coral Crown. Their opening economies and household partitions are equal; decision routes, consent delays and council gates differ. All names, institutions, party bodies, statistics and marker locations are fictional test content. The real-land backdrop supplies no fictional borders or sovereignty. No historical observations or external datasets are used.

`python3 scripts/build_synthetic_world.py` produces the pack deterministically from explicit definitions. `--check` compares the committed output without writing. Both clients accept it through `--content`; the terminal also needs `--country AUR` (or BOR/COR). The recovery example accepts `--content data/synthetic-test.json` and tests the same five-year crisis objective through ordinary commands. Synthetic tests establish rule consistency; they do not establish historical accuracy or real-world calibration.

## Dated identities and bookmarks

Content schema 2 now accepts optional `identities` and country `governing_office` fields. The catalogue contains stable typed person/office IDs, office roles and existence intervals, office-holder terms and primary-currency intervals. Every record includes a source ID, source version and an explicit quality class: observed, reconciled, interpolated, inferred, authored or unknown. Names can change independently of IDs. Reference office roles do not grant command powers; the existing government configuration still defines playable authority.

Intervals include `valid_from` and exclude `valid_to`; a null end is open-ended. Terms cannot precede or outlast their office's existence. Overlapping incumbencies, conflicting primary currencies, missing references, duplicate IDs/sources and invalid dates fail assembly. Collective holders list explicit members; vacancy is recorded separately from an optional office with no known incumbent. A required active office must have a person, collective or explicit vacancy. This does not yet implement full appointment/succession law, personnel AI, country founding/dissolution, memberships or territorial transitions.

The loader assembles the governing name/title and currency for `Content.start`. `Content::at_bookmark` recompiles those fields for another supported date and validates the result. A campaign branches from this assembly: later reference terms and currency changes are not automatically enacted. Save content hashes prevent silently replacing a campaign's starting records. Optional fields are omitted for the original pack, preserving its rules-2 world checksum and canonical world encoding.

Civil reference dates use the Gregorian calendar, years 0001–9999. Campaign starts range from 2000-01-01 through 2095-01-01, allowing a five-calendar-year review by the existing 2100-01-01 limit. February 29 anniversaries use February 28 in a non-leap year. Ticks retain the 2000 epoch; later starts initialize fiscal execution at the bookmark through the next January boundary and offset authored election/leadership timers. Reporting and AI reviews retain their authored 30-day cadence. Annual calibration observations accept years up to the bookmark year; exact publication-date visibility and full time-series assembly remain incomplete.

`synthetic-dated-test.json`, generated by the same offline script, starts on 2001-07-15. Aurora has a head of government, collective head of state, vacant speaker and unknown auditor. An intentionally one-day reference term tests exact handover boundaries; it is entirely fictional. The fixture also supplies a future primary-currency identity. Neither reference change forces a running campaign. No dates or people in this fixture are historical claims, and the original 15 countries have not received invented office-term histories.

## Relative-price rules and fixtures

`synthetic-market-test.json` is an authored, reproducibly generated three-government fixture using content schema 3 and explicit rules 3. Earlier packs omit `rules`, default to rules 2 and retain their canonical content/world encodings. A rules-3 pack requires market definitions; rules 2 reject them. This prevents older clients from silently treating the new fixture as a fixed-price game. The original classic pack remains unchanged. The preserved 15-country market variant uses the same country/policy/fact definitions, with market assumptions attributed separately to `authored-markets-v1`.

Stable typed good IDs identify food and energy definitions independently of names. Units, price bounds, maximum daily relative adjustment, import caps, storage ceilings, food spoilage and the food share of essential baskets are content-defined and source-tagged as authored. Energy storage loss is explicitly zero in this version; network losses require a later model. Food and energy remain distinct quantity types. A relative price of 1.00 is the opening index, not an observed currency price or CPI.

Settlement uses yesterday's announced index. Food reprices the configured part of household baskets, and both prices scale bounded import bills. Supply versus affordable demand determines the following day's index, with capped proportional adjustments and downward integer rounding. Physical need, requested use and delivered consumption remain distinct. Food requests reflect household affordability; energy requests are physical industrial need, with cash settlement modeled for imports only. Price changes neither create money nor rewrite earlier transactions. The record reconciles opening stock plus production/imports to delivered use, explicit loss and closing stock. Existing supply, fiscal, consent, political and AI phases remain ordered; full sector recipes, wage-setting, general inflation and banking remain absent.

Rules-3 snapshots require format 4; rules-2 snapshots continue to write format 3. Supported older snapshots resolve to the classic bundled data when no explicit pack is pinned and retain their original files until explicitly saved/restored. A converted recovery backup is uniquely archived before later backup rotation can overwrite it. Save conversion archives originals by their format number; incompatible rules or content are rejected rather than repricing an old campaign. Autosave and default manual filenames distinguish formats.

## Financing, constitutional and commitment fixtures

`synthetic-finance-test.json` (rules/content 4) adds authored borrowing routes, consent delays and emergency costs. `synthetic-constitution-test.json` (rules/content 5) adds eight authored governing templates, lawful/contested transitions and fictional AI preferences. The rules-5/6 starters use the same transition definitions with separately attributed provenance and preferences for each opening regime. These are gameplay assumptions, not historical constitutional or debt-market facts.

`synthetic-commitment-test.json` (rules/content 6, snapshot format 7) adds authored amendment administration costs, per-proposal amendment limits and observed-review intervals. The fixture retains its fictional country/party names and data; the default rules-6 starter uses the same procedures with separate authored provenance. Paid amendments, withdrawal with sunk costs and programme history are simulated outcomes derived from ordinary commands. Outcome reviews do not turn observed before/after differences into historical facts or causal estimates. All fixtures are rebuilt offline by `build_synthetic_world.py` and validated with `--check`; source versions and exact content hashes distinguish each model.
